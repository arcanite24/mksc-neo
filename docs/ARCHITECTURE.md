# Architecture

MKSC Neo has one application shell and a recovered native C simulation; the
3D course engine has a separate sanctioned simulation. SDL3 supplies backends.
## Source map
| Directory | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `portable/core/` | Fixed-tick physics, contacts, terrain, camera and effect controllers. |
| `portable/app/` | Frontend, race sessions, CPU roster, items, scoring and ceremony. |
| `portable/assets/` | Bounded pack readers, composed content and borrowed typed views. |
| `portable/import/` | Owner-ROM bytes to a deterministic pack in memory. |
| `portable/audio/` | Sequencer, PCM/PSG voices and mixing. |
| `portable/platform/` | Entry point, SDL services, shell, renderers, input, files and Lua host. |
| `portable/tests/` | Unit tests, format fixtures and real executable routes. |
| `tools/portable/` | Checks, reference importer, mod kit and packaging. |
| `mods/examples/` | Editable scripts and generators; generated game assets stay local. |
## Simulation and application flow
The scheduler admits fixed GBA ticks: 4389/262144 seconds, approximately 59.7275 Hz, with at most four catch-up ticks per host frame. Input is sampled once per host frame. Pausing or losing focus stops tick admission and audio. Reconnection requires neutral input and confirmation before resuming.

Android lifecycle watches publish atomic flags only; the active leaf host consumes them on the game thread before admitting ticks. Backgrounding clears held input, pauses races and audio, and resets scheduler debt. Foregrounding keeps a race paused until an explicit resume. Android Back follows the active menu owner or opens the race pause menu; repeated key events are ignored. Optional touch controls aggregate fingers by device and finger identity into the existing frontend and Player 1 driving masks. Quick taps retain menu edges until a presentation tick consumes them; Hop and Item taps separately survive until the next admitted live race tick. Their cached transparent overlay is drawn after ordinary presentation and released before its SDL renderer.

The shared shell owns begin/event/frame/end lifecycles. Startup pack validation, mod loading and candidate preparation use an exclusive SDL worker while a pack-free loading surface (the modern menu loading card: cached still layer, live step list and segmented progress) pumps events and presents; joined candidates publish on main. The first-run version check hands its verified bytes to launch, and the startup window/renderer transfer to the shell. Single-threaded web builds retain synchronous preparation. GP, Time Trial, Quick Run, Battle and local multiplayer use the same recovered driver, contact and item systems. Developer routes feed ordinary inputs through these owners. Local multiplayer advances one world, then publishes each player's view.

Simulation uses fixed-width integers and explicit fixed-point, wrapping and shift operations. Preserve original update order, RNG draws and tick counts. Route geometry uses integer comparisons and bearings. Rendering, audio resampling and optional Lua math do not feed stock simulation arithmetic. Unsupported states report their mode, course and stage instead of succeeding with placeholder behavior.

Snapshots publish camera, racers, actors, effects and audio requests. Rendering never advances a controller or writes simulation. Optional interpolation blends previous/current snapshots; phase changes, recovery, restarts and large jumps snap to current state. Authored sprite cells, texture-pattern keys, visibility, palette flashes, HUD and audio remain on the simulation tick. Fractional sky/weather companions are presentation data; the original integer fields remain available for native-resolution sampling.

## Ownership and backend boundary
`core/`, `app/`, `assets/` and `audio/` require neither SDL nor a filesystem. Backends supply clocks, semantic input, diagnostics, presentation and optional persistence. `portable_diagnostic_printf` is the diagnostic adapter; desktop hosts write stderr. Memory replay streams and optional FILE adapters share the same codecs and checksum rules.

Sessions are caller-owned and simulation does not allocate. Keep large owners in checked heap storage or an arena. Constructors initialize storage in place; a failed destination must be discarded or reinitialized. Input views must not alias the destination. Keep decoded content and owners stationary while views borrow their buffers. Platform cleanup releases resources once, including after partial initialization. Browser callbacks retain the pack until cancellation.

All wire readers decode bytes explicitly. Native pointers, padding and struct layout are excluded from files and replay hashes. Bound numeric text before narrowing; choose floor versus truncation deliberately for signed arithmetic. Host file readers reject inputs outside their supported size before conversion. Mod content has smaller independent limits described in [Modding](MODDING.md).

Backends initialize a recovered TT/GP/local session from its content view, admit fixed input ticks, then publish immutable snapshots. Presentation rate never advances physics; session APIs live in `portable/app/`.

## Rendering and audio

Source art uses a logical 240×160 coordinate space. Race surfaces own 1×–8× physical buffers; widescreen
extends the logical width and camera coverage. Left/center/right native views retain the parent stride. Auto
aspect replaces buffers on resize while retaining the scene's simulation and content owners.

Road perspective is evaluated per physical row. Transformed karts, scenery, items and effects sample each
physical pixel; available kart art is selected by physical footprint. Sky, road and visible world sprites
share the prior camera's latched draw offset, matching the original cached actor positions. Enhanced
projection retains the road's physical-row phase; simulation keeps its current camera state. Moving sky,
snow, lightning and minimap markers retain fractional placement at higher resolutions. The art's texels
remain authored pixels. The results portrait bank is imported after the overlapping name bank, as in the
original; its shared border tiles frame all stock and custom interiors. Native 1× retains original sampling.
A stable depth queue orders world actors and karts; HUD and menu layers use their own anchors and
compositing.

Renderers borrow immutable decoded content. Ambient palette/tile changes apply to draw-local views and
scratch buffers, so rendering cannot alter base assets or the next simulation turn. Results retain the live
race behind transparent panels; menus and ceremony have their own imported motion controllers. Modern canvas
art and font scaling use floating point: ARM and x86 can differ at raster edges. Compare those captures
against the matching architecture; fixed-snapshot race-world pixels and normalized replay records retain
exact comparisons.

Wide UI extends existing patterned/affine backgrounds with centered signed coordinates and existing
clocks/fades. Foregrounds retain native anchors. Title wings borrow owner-ROM sky/cloud art in a bounded 8
MiB presentation cache, reflecting sky edges and continuing pavement without duplicating drivers/logo.
Explicit mod layers win; failed imports preserve the previous cache. No gameplay pack identity changes. Flat
title animation remains identical for each variant.

Artwork Detail travels with each presentation framebuffer, including split views, resizes, ghosts and live
GP results backgrounds. Original retains source selection and G39 physical sampling. High selects an
authored size meeting the physical footprint with one available tier of headroom; Max selects the highest
available size. Geometry, anchors, palettes and animation ownership remain with the source cell. Sparse and
equal-size alternatives retain their valid fallback; single-art items/effects have no alternate detail. The
option never enters pack identity, simulation state or replay codecs.

The optional 3D renderer stays in the existing race stack. Each race host owns a reusable CPU triangle/depth
workspace; split views reuse it sequentially. The flat course samples the complete immutable world map,
including ambient palettes and transparent texels. Its vertices use the source integer camera basis and
shared physical draw offset; the original two-row affine road convention remains confined to Original
rendering. Perspective UV/depth, near/far and viewport clipping are evaluated at physical resolution. Large
ground quads may use seven persistent row workers with disjoint clips. Jobs reuse the same immutable
triangle kernel and join before any foreground or resource reuse. Small buffers, web and unavailable
threading submit serially. Billboards retain source poses, anchors and authored coverage. Their own depth
plane orders world sprites without cutting wheels or shadows that extend below the source ground anchor.
Ground writes geometry depth; billboards write object depth; model fragments test both planes before
updating either. This preserves mixed opaque/cutout occlusion without a third depth buffer. HUD, weather and
screen-space effects keep source compositing. Frame depth borrows end on every success/failure path, before
UI or framebuffer reuse. Unsupported projections fall back visibly to Original; malformed map/tile data fail
rather than silently replacing content.

Optional model catalogs decode bounded mesh/material/binding sections once per race host and borrow the
effective pack until that host closes. Driver bindings use manifest identity, including custom ghosts and
local seats; scenery uses course identity and the placement's normalized base art bank. Pose variants select
exact source overrides, with the original billboard as the fallback. Model transforms read full
source-facing yaw before sprite quantization, source hop height, affine effects and cached bob. These
presentation fields do not enter simulation hashes or replay codecs. Original ignores model sections.
Invalid optional rows warn and retain unrelated gameplay; malformed pack containers still fail ordinary
validation. M13 keeps legacy BND1 kind98/id1/version1 replacement. Opt-in version2 uses the same header/44-byte rows: normalize per accepted 2D/3D layer into separately owned v1 registries, replacing all predecessor rows with matching four-word keys, stable-sorting and retaining raw invalid/duplicate winners for catalog-local fallback. Enforce256 raw rows and charge payload/section tables against the existing combined budget before publication; failure releases both domain candidates. Never inherit from rejected composition scratch. Normalized identities include inherited rows; legacy nonoverlay identities stay unchanged. Raw version2 bases fail explicitly. Static 3D OBJ integer `mksc:scenery-art-bank` selects pose0 by course/bank; compose binding scale/pivot before the OBJ affine without driver unit conversion and cache immutable geometry by mesh ID. Missing/invalid replacements retain required explicit model/billboard; controllers, coins and skies reject this selector.

Local VS/Battle publishes one presentation history per player only after the shared world accepts a complete
tick. Drawing reads those endpoints, reuses the ordinary interpolation helpers and one sequential 3D
workspace, and never selects or advances a simulation player. Saved fixed aspect widths apply per pane; Auto
derives a bounded width from each pane's output area. Composite pixels and textures are replaced
transactionally on resize, with the parent physical stride retained by every pane and centered native HUD
subview. Render deadlines control extra presents independently of fixed simulation ticks.

Modern menus (`platform/modern_menu*.c`) present the same `PortableFrontendFlow` state at window resolution:
a CPU `UiCanvas` (analytic anti-aliased shapes, bilinear images), SDF text from embedded Nunito fonts via
stb_truetype, and a pack art cache that EPX-smooths illustrations while sprites stay nearest. Layout uses a
1920×1080 design stage scaled uniformly; backgrounds and bars span any aspect. Pages read flow fields only
and animate entrances from the flow tick, so they imply short transitions; input, timing and events stay
with the flow and shell. Display names come from `data/menu-names.json` (embedded), with pack name art as
the fallback for content it does not list. The first-start screens use the same renderer before any pack
exists. ROM and ZIP choices share the bounded importer; starting the game from first-start preferences
requires successful atomic settings replacement. Failed saves retain edited values for retry. Modern race
results borrow immutable times, ranks, point transfers and action rows from the existing
TT/GP/Quick/VS/Battle controllers. One clean physical race image is retained across each result sequence and
resized for presentation; ghost prompts, failure dialogs and cumulative standings share the same canvas.
Their animation clocks advance only on admitted presentation ticks. Classic boards, action dispatch, scoring
and replay simulation retain their own owners. Modern handoff cards present course catalog art before
synchronous race setup, then reveal the race during the first 48 existing entry/countdown ticks. Results
reuse the first 16 admitted result ticks while the revealed board continues its own entrance animation;
ceremonies reuse their music and exit clocks. Hosts supply elapsed progress, so repeated renders and window
exposes cannot advance a card or add simulation ticks. Classic fades are suppressed only in modern draw
views, leaving the source owners and skip acceptance unchanged.

Simulation emits ordered audio commands and event serials. The audio session applies source player
priorities, sequences and voice lifecycles. Platform audio selects cues, mixes engine/scene output and
bounds the SDL queue. Mesh races share that queue through immutable accepted-tick audio views; generation changes preflight/rebind the mixer to 3D content and exit restores 2D menu content. SI speed becomes a dimensionless engine pitch ratio. Pause clears queued output; repeated ordinals cannot replay cues or mix PCM. Missing META music means silence between cues. DS songs (kind 112) play on `audio/nds_sequence.c`, a native NNS-style sequencer at the DS sound frame (~192 Hz) with DS ADSR/LFO/sweep/portamento, channel stealing and linear interpolation, mixed after the GBA output; START_SONG flag 0x8000 selects them, and pause, tempo, volume and fade route to it. The MKDS importer converts each course theme once from `sound_data.sdat` (SSEQ + used SBNK programs + used SWAR waves); the course→sequence table is byte 1 of the US ARM9 sound-set rows at 0x0215316C, indexed by internal course ID, and lives in `mkds_import.c`. N64 songs (kind 115, START_SONG 0x4000..0x7fff) play on `audio/n64_sequence.c`, a native port of the MK64 (SM64 "EU") sound driver: M64 sequence/channel/layer scripts at the original tatum rate (three updates per tick, tempo limit 10770), ADSR, vibrato, portamento, note stealing over 24 notes, streaming VADPCM with loop states, linear interpolation and one delay-line reverb. Effects (0x2000 | bank << 8 | sound) restart the one-player effect sequence and request the sound through its channel IO ports as the game's sound manager does; a song's `follow` field chains a finish fanfare into its results theme. A sound set's final-lap jingle holds the stock final-lap wait until it ends, then the course theme restarts at 125% tempo.

## Content and persistence

The native importer verifies the US ROM and emits the same pack bytes as the Python reference. Files and import dialogs belong to platform; the importer works on supplied byte buffers. Imported rules, art, music and catalogs drive the runtime. Packs carry an importer stamp so the shell can offer reimport.

Content layers replace sections by `(kind, content_id)` in explicit mod order. Decoders validate lengths, ranges and references before publishing views. Composition reuses accepted hashes and compares unchanged deterministic section streams directly, preserving saved identities; ordered pack bounds checks use a linear fast path, and GCC/Clang Debug builds optimize the pack hashing reader. Data overrides normalize against earlier layers and commit only after validation.
The mod manager discovers bounded manifests and stages an explicit order; one-step toggles, installs and imports place a mod by its dependency rules and publish without review, restoring the active order on failure. Strict preparation owns composed content, validated Lua and fresh menu/audio owners without publishing identity. After preparation (and review, in Advanced load order), atomic settings replacement
precedes a non-failing owner transfer at the shell iteration boundary; failed
validation or saving leaves active owners intact. SDL resources stay stationary.
Modern Options exposes this controller in Mods and the Games library; per-category asset priorities compose before candidate publication. Its immutable
view borrows the pending manager; category switches preserve staged edits and
leaving the manager or Options asks Apply/Discard/Keep editing. Modern order actions and word-aware diagnostic
pages share keyboard, gamepad and touch adapters; Classic keeps its manager.
Archive review owns exact bounded ZIP bytes, checks every entry's inflation/CRC,
and validates composed content/Lua through a borrowed source override in the same
loader. Shell preflight decodes every selectable course/driver pair and renders
menu pages without advancing simulation. Confirmation writes an exclusive sibling temporary file and atomically
replaces an unchanged destination. Installation never publishes settings or live
content; updates validate staged, running and persisted selections. Dialog callbacks
retain only a ref-counted request; polling and publication run on the main thread.
The primary mod root plus four remembered extras share an ID resolver: cross-root duplicates reject that ID, same-root folders precede ZIPs, and paths never enter fingerprints. Managers and install transactions own their root strings; installs write root zero and validate across all roots. Android SAF roots resolve children through DocumentsContract with persistent read grants; picked files use bounded SDL streams without seeking.
Reload/F5 refresh discovery and prepare replacement owners before atomic save/publication; decoded menu art is cleared before retiring old owners, preventing reused-address cache hits. Optional menu-only watching polls bounded source metadata every 250 ms with 500 ms debounce, suspends during races, recordings, dialogs and staged edits, and reuses that transaction. SAF roots use explicit Reload/F5 because provider metadata is not a reliable watcher.
Lua states live in platform and communicate through `app/race_script_hooks.h`.
Each mod/race has its own sandbox and deterministic random stream.

Saves, ghosts and replays use explicit versioned codecs and content identity. Modded records are separated from stock; incompatible ghosts are rejected. Mirror mode has separate records/progress. File replacement occurs only after validation and complete serialization, so failed writes preserve prior files.
Drift Extras selects a 2D reward layer before user mods, preserving original handling. Drift Tiers (historical Modern) owns separate per-kart drift state and adds six fields only to version67 replays; Off retains Original version66 bytes/hashes. Reward-profile compatibility hashes a profile domain with the content digest, preventing cross-profile ghosts even when mod-authored rule IDs coincide. Profile switching validates target progress without publishing global identity, then transfers prepared owners and selects its fingerprinted save namespace.

E8/E9 (Drift Extras) remain recovered-2D-only profiles. T1 introduces integer-only modern 3D
physics inspired by [Kinoko](https://github.com/vabold/Kinoko/tree/550c97a),
pinned to `550c97a`: KartMove's acceleration/filtering/facing separation,
KartBoost's independent clocks and KartCollide's normal/friction impulses.
Its external vehicle files do not supply freely reusable coefficient tables;
author bounded tuning data rather than claiming exact MK8 statistics. Adapt
its 59.94 Hz dynamics to our fixed 59.7275 Hz tick. Copied/adapted code or tables
retain the complete [MIT notice](https://github.com/vabold/Kinoko/blob/550c97a/LICENSE)
in `LICENSE-NOTICE.md` and shipped notices. This adaptation is being replaced with rules recovered from MK8 Deluxe v1.0: one MK8 frame per tick, 1 MK8 unit = 0.0526 m, MK8 formulas and engine constants, and our own authored per-stat-row KPH values.

## Imported game content

Other games' content comes only from the player's own ROM, converted inside `mksc` at run time; nothing derived is committed or shipped. Each imported game is a content platform: the mod providing 3D courses (kind 99) that also provides DRV3 rows (the shell asks the mod set which load order provides a section). Its drivers get their own frontend pages with `PortableFrontendDriverSlot.platform` = load order + 1; locking one sets `flow.platform`, which filters course groups and later players' pages, and the 3D host draws rivals only from that platform's roster (`portable_course_3d_host_set_roster`). MK64 (US) uses `portable/import/n64/`: byte-order normalization, full SHA-1, bounded MIO0 and compact F3DEX conversion into the shared C3D1/TXB1 writer. Static segment-7 faces and source collision lists convert at 17.6 source units/metre (each primary path over the game's stated course length; vtxStretchY stays 1.0). Race courses get the shared modern physics profile, three laps, gates every 18 m of primary path (parallel gates where alternative paths branch), a respawn per gate, CPU routes over all four paths, the spawn_players.c GP grid, source water levels as META `mksc:fall-height`, the four source cups and source item boxes (8.66 units above their spawns) with MK64's own item odds: gen_random_item's segment-13 curves (100 item bytes per rank) become MIR1 weights for GP humans/CPUs, VS (the 4P curve; its CPUs keep the GP CPU curve) and battle (one curve), selected per mode by META `mksc:item-rules`/`mksc:item-rules-vs`. Item art binds per profile as PORTABLE_MODEL_ITEM rows: shell billboards from the eight green and four blue CI8 frames (red is the green TLUT with red and green swapped), banana, fake box, item box and a shard from segment-13 display lists, and the item window pictures as HUD icons. Packed lists take the texture state their segment-6 callers set (only the fields set; segment-6 state lists followed); cutout and translucent faces follow opaque ones. Section lists repeat surfaces with other triangulations (the game draws only the camera's section), so a face whose 21 interior samples all lie within 8 cm of earlier same-layer faces (normals within 0.95) is dropped; faces then split at T-junctions (1 cm), and cutout/translucent corners lift 1.5 cm along their face's dominant axis (under the 2 cm kart shadow) except where uncovered opaque ground meets them, so no crack opens. Vertex alpha and colour come from init_course's find_vtx_and_set_colours lists (a per-course table; the ROM's fourth vertex byte is ignored as the game sets it), and lists it marks for the water pass blend translucent. `mk64_object.c` tables name where scenery lives: actor families (trees, signs, cacti, palms, still piranha plants) as OBJS meshes, convoys (Kalimari trains, DK ferry) on the generate_2d_path resampling, Toad's Turnpike traffic on path 0 lanes Koopa crabs and Yoshi Valley hedgehogs, and Bowser's Castle Thwomps (the 150cc ThwompSpawn list; each behaviour 1-6 becomes a tick-driven loop of hover, slam, patrol or slide legs, since motion cannot wait for a kart), all route-moving hazard spheres (vehicles on one path and speed share one 256-node route and differ by `mksc:motion-phase`, so trains stay coupled). Every collidable family names its `mksc:hazard-reaction`: vehicles and the Yoshi egg launch the kart, crabs, hedgehogs and piranha plants spin it, Thwomps squash it; source objects advance once a frame, two engine ticks (FRAME_TICKS), so their per-frame speeds, rests and clips are halved or doubled accordingly. Armature objects (Chain Chomps, Yoshi Valley flags, Sherbet Land penguins, Koopa Troopa Beach seagulls) read the source limb lists and animation channels into one looping RIG1 clip; random or kart-triggered behaviour becomes a deterministic schedule (penguin sliders pre-simulated, Choco Mountain rocks bounced on the collision faces, walls included, and gone after 360 frames; Moo Moo moles popping per hole group; Banshee Boardwalk's two Boo flocks flying their splines around their trigger points). Sprite families (snowmen, moles, bomb karts, Boos, neon signs, train and ferry smoke, Bowser's Castle fire breath) are camera-facing quads whose frames or palettes are RIG1 nodes shown per tick; placements sharing a rigged mesh with a nonzero `mksc:motion-phase` get their own skin posed at that phase.
